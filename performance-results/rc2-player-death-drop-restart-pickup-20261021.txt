A fresh disposable `rc2-death-audit` world was used with the exact published RC2 JARs. An offline protocol-777 player joined through the built-in proxy in Survival. `/mpdebug` reported `smoke-b` for the player's chunk. Backend B's RCON set `keep_inventory=false` (26.3 gamerule spelling `keep_inventory`), gave the player exactly seven emeralds, then issued `/kill` on that owner backend. The player inventory became empty, and one tagged emerald item entity appeared with count 7 and UUID `[I; -1829853031, -1829548174, -1150141977, 524334602]`.

After the other backend loaded the death chunk, both RCON views reported the same item tag, UUID, and count. Both backends completed `save-all flush`, stopped cleanly, and restarted with the same Master/world directories. After the chunk was force-loaded again, each backend still reported the same seven-emerald entity UUID and tag, and both lists showed zero players online. The death chunk used explicit force-load tickets; this is a clean save/restart persistence check, not an unload/reload or abrupt-crash check.

The player rejoined after restart in the death state. The protocol client sent the 26.3 respawn command; the server sent a respawn packet and health returned to 20. RCON then teleported the disposable player to the dropped item. Both backends reported exactly seven emeralds in the player's inventory and no longer found the tagged item entity. This verifies one commanded Survival death drop, replicated entity continuity across a clean Master/backend restart, and pickup without leaving the tested copy behind.

The receiver's code finds no chunk holder and makes no subscriber-set mutation in this case. This run's later cross-backend item observation and pickup succeeded; the high warning volume remains a logging/diagnostic issue to classify and reduce. Windows OSHI performance-counter errors also appeared. No duplicate-UUID warnings were found in the retained death-test backend logs. All disposable processes and ports were stopped/closed.
