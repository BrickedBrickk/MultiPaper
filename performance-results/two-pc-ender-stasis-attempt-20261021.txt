# Two-PC ender pearl stasis probe — inconclusive — 2026-10-21

## Scope and artifacts

Disposable two-PC pair using the current Paperclip SHA-256 `3D046088BE2610B19F3B64A99496BBEB05FA52ED8660D46D7762F47307D61D3D` on both PCs and Master SHA-256 `BD112490769F0B0037D4772CF02C88317B8F38F504579F1A66B1A73049DD6968`; Temurin JRE 25.0.4.1. PC1 ran Master/Backend A, PC2 ran Backend B. The player logged in through the isolated built-in proxy. SSH used the saved host keys with strict checking. No production world, service, or Crafty access.

## Attempt

Backend B owned the force-loaded test chunk. It contained a soul-sand bubble column from y=61 through y=89 and a waterlogged oak trapdoor at y=90; Backend B's RCON conditional checks confirmed `bubble_column` at `(10005,89,10005)` and a waterlogged trapdoor at `(10005,90,10005)`. A survival protocol client received water breathing, stood at `(10005.5,60,10009.5)` facing yaw -180/pitch -35, received one ender pearl, and sent the normal `use_item` packet.

The pearl entity spawned. Sequential RCON samples briefly found the same UUID on both backends, but the recorded positions differed by about 3.4 blocks in Z while it was moving. A later query found no pearl and the player had been displaced below the test floor. The sample timing and aim did not establish whether the pearl entered the intended holding position. Source inspection confirms that the 26.3 `ServerboundUseItemPacket` fields are ordered `yRot`, then `xRot`, and `handleUseItem` applies that yaw and pitch before projectile launch, matching the probe packet field order. The run did not record projectile `Motion` immediately after spawn, so it does not verify stasis or stable cross-backend position and does not establish a MultiPaper defect. A follow-up should capture the same entity UUID, `Pos`, and `Motion` immediately on both backends, then sample again after it should reach the holding area.

An earlier throw with a solid glass cap teleported the player into water and caused drowning; that geometry was invalid for a stasis hold. The player was protected with water breathing for the waterlogged-trapdoor attempt. Both disposable backends saved and stopped cleanly, followed by the Master (`Exiting safely`); all private test ports closed.

## Disposition

The initial player-throw attempt was inconclusive because it missed the holding area. The controlled ownerless and player-owned follow-ups above verify short-term stasis and one collision release across both physical PCs. Long-duration hold, chunk unload/reload, restart, redstone-triggered release, and Mojang-client behavior remain unverified.
## Controlled stationary-pearl follow-up — partial pass

To isolate the chamber and replication from aiming, Backend B summoned an ownerless ender pearl at `(12005.5,80.5,12005.5)` with zero initial motion. The test chunk used a soul-sand bubble column from y=60 through y=89 and a waterlogged oak trapdoor at y=90 with `half=top`. RCON confirmed the bubble-column block at y=89. Over two seconds, the pearl rose to y=87.42 with upward motion. It then remained in the top pocket near y=90 for 12 seconds: Backend B positions ranged from y=89.953 to 90.064; Backend A positions ranged from y=89.969 to 90.080. Both reported UUID `[I; -106125561, 1217085579, -1279399334, 519852209]`, with x/z fixed at 12005.5. The sampled vertical motion varied around zero, between approximately -0.033 and +0.093. Backend A saw the entity after an automated client loaded the chunk. The pair saved and stopped cleanly after the sample.

This is evidence that the top-half waterlogged-trapdoor chamber holds a pearl entity and synchronizes it between the two PCs. The entity was summoned without an owner, so this does not verify a normal player-thrown pearl, player ownership, release/teleport behavior, or a Mojang client. A separate protocol `use_item` attempt consumed the test pearl but produced no entity in the first RCON sample; it remains unresolved and is not counted as a pass. Overall stasis verification remains partial.
## Player-owned two-PC stasis and release — 2026-10-21

A final controlled run used the same exact current artifacts and Java 25.0.4.1 on PC1 (Master/Backend A) and PC2 (Backend B). Backend B owned a fresh force-loaded test chunk with soul sand at `(16005,59,16005)`, source water from y=60 through y=89, and a waterlogged top-half oak trapdoor at `(16005,90,16005)`. RCON confirmed the bubble column at y=80. An offline 26.3 protocol client connected through the built-in proxy, received water breathing and one ender pearl, stood at `(16005.5,60,16005.5)`, and sent a normal `use_item` packet facing straight up.

The client received an ender-pearl spawn packet for UUID `a41c097e-9617-44ce-89a6-f1fc7195737d`, starting at y=61.52 with upward velocity 1.48996. Backend A and Backend B both reported the same pearl UUID and the same `Owner` UUID int array as the player's UUID: `[I; 2102583172, -2101726802, -1117129794, 2139907785]`. The player remained connected. Across six samples over about eight seconds, the pearl stayed at the top pocket near y=90 with effectively zero horizontal motion. Backend A's y readings were 90.010; Backend B's ranged 89.997–90.010. The coordinate difference between backends was below 0.02 blocks in these stable samples.

To test release, Backend B replaced the waterlogged trapdoor block at y=90 with stone. Within 300 ms the pearl was gone and both backends reported the owner at the pearl location around `(16005.5075,89.9966,16005.5683)`, up from the starting y=60. The stone was then removed; both backends still listed the player online. Both backends accepted `save-all flush` and stopped cleanly; the Master logged `Exiting safely`; the private test ports were closed.

This verifies one player-thrown pearl entering a stasis hold, ownership replication, short-term cross-PC position agreement, and collision-triggered release. The release used a direct block replacement, not a redstone circuit. It does not test hours-long holds, chunk unload/reload, server restart while held, redstone activation, or a Mojang launcher client.
