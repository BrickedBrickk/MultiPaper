### Follow-up observer control

A fresh disposable fixture on the same exact jars directly summoned a long-lived tagged area-effect-cloud in the Nether on `portal-a`. Both backends returned the same UUID for that entity. However, the same UUID was also returned by queries wrapped in `execute in minecraft:overworld`, so the RCON query pattern used here did not independently prove the entity's dimension. The portal frames were also aligned at the same coordinates in both dimensions, allowing the pig to potentially re-enter a portal after its cooldown. These probe-design limitations further prevent treating the earlier readout as evidence of a product defect or a verified portal transfer. A revised probe must use dimension-distinguishing checks, non-bouncing portal geometry, and time-bounded UUID reads immediately at transfer.

A no-portal control then force-loaded the same coordinates in both dimensions and summoned only a tagged area-effect-cloud in the Nether. Distinct block conditions verified that `execute in minecraft:overworld` and `execute in minecraft:the_nether` did select different dimensions on both backends. Yet `execute in <dimension> if entity @e[tag=...]` returned true for the Nether-only control on both dimensions and both backends. The command-selector check therefore cannot serve as a per-dimension entity-membership assertion in this fixture; an in-process per-world entity-list check is needed before diagnosing duplicate entities or portal transfer behavior.
