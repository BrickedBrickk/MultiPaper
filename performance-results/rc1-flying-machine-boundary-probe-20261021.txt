RC1 two-way flying machine chunk-boundary probe
Date: 2026-10-21

Result: The documented six-block Java two-way Engine A moved across the x=160
chunk edge in a disposable two-backend run. Both backends reported all six
blocks at the same final coordinates. The run does not independently prove
the two adjacent chunks had different owners, so treat this as chunk-boundary
replication evidence, not a conclusive cross-owner gameplay pass.

Fixture and artifacts
---------------------
The fixture was `%TEMP%\multipaper-26.3-flying-machine-jdk25-20261021`, with
disposable world `fm_disposable_20261021`, Master ports 35795/27820, backend
game ports 27821/27822, and RCON ports 27831/27832. Both Paperclip JARs had
SHA-256 `B31E80322C7A980D42D1D91932551DA406C939DD167F9C795F856C73FDC456F2`;
the Master had SHA-256
`BD112490769F0B0037D4772CF02C88317B8F38F504579F1A66B1A73049DD6968`. The
pair ran under Temurin JRE 25.0.3.9. No production world, live process, or
Crafty panel was accessed.

Test and observations
---------------------
The schematic used two diagonal slime blocks, two opposing sticky pistons,
and two observers above the slime blocks facing up (output down). For the
boundary run, the slime blocks began at `(159,100,12000)` and
`(160,100,12001)`. The pistons were at `(160,100,12000)` facing west and
`(159,100,12001)` facing east; observers were at y=101 above the slime blocks.
The chunks are x=9 and x=10. Backend A force-loaded x=9 first and Backend B
force-loaded x=10 first, then both loaded both chunks. This was intended to
assign different owners, but an `/mpdebug` owner-overlay sample was not
obtained; the owner split is therefore unverified.

Both backends initially confirmed all six blocks. A block update in the
observer's detection face started the engine. After it ran, both backends
reported the complete machine at x=169–170, with the same two slime blocks,
two sticky pistons, and two observers. This is a 10-block eastward movement
from the x=159–160 layout, across the x=160 chunk boundary, with no block loss
visible in either backend view.

An identical single-chunk control at x=200–201 also activated and retained
all six blocks; both backends later reported the translated layout at
x=200–202. This establishes that the chosen schematic and trigger worked in
the control run.

Limits and cleanup
------------------
An attempted protocol-client `/mpdebug` probe could not decode the 26.3
client-information packet using the available 26.1 protocol schema. Thus,
although chunks 9 and 10 were first force-loaded by different backends, their
final ownership was not independently confirmed. No vanilla launcher GUI
client was used. The result does not test longer-duration operation, obstacles,
or other engine designs. Reverse travel remains inconclusive; see the follow-up
below.

Both backends were save-flushed and stopped cleanly, then the disposable
Master was stopped. Ports 35795, 27820–27822, and 27831–27832 were confirmed
closed. The temporary fixture was retained for audit.

Reverse-direction follow-up
---------------------------
The saved disposable fixture was restarted with the same exact JARs. With the
engine at x=169–170, RCON placed stone in the second observer's detection face
at `(170,102,12001)` for about 150 ms, removed it, and let both backends tick
for two seconds. Both backends still reported the complete machine at the same
coordinates. The observer's powered state was not sampled during the pulse,
so this is an inconclusive trigger attempt, not evidence that reverse travel
works or fails. The pair was flushed and stopped; all fixture listeners were
confirmed closed again.

Owner-assignment follow-up
--------------------------
A temporary reflection-only plugin queried `MultiPaper.getChunkHolder()` on
the exact RC1 pair in a fresh disposable world. After the RCON `forceload add`
requests, both backends had loaded holders for chunks 9 and 10, but both
reported `externalOwner=null` through the observation window, including after
ticks were unfrozen. No player was connected. This confirms that the earlier
force-load ordering did not create a two-owner test; the flying-engine run
proves movement and replica agreement across a chunk-coordinate boundary
only. The server-side probe files and logs remain in the disposable fixture;
the temporary plugin and its configuration changes were removed/restored.
